# tsukamae

A personal living dex tracker, running as a Windows desktop app (Electron). Forked from [pokedextracker.com](https://pokedextracker.com) and stripped down to a local-contained, offline, and single-user app with additional marking capabilities to reflect continuous and fluid progression. Version 2.0 keeps a full record of each mon, entered one at a time in the Review and sealed once it's verified, and reads the collection back in scenes of its own.

## Additional Capabilities

Beyond the upstream caught/uncaught toggle, each slot tracks how it's held and where the mon came from:

- **Capture status** — every marked slot is one of:
  - _Caught_ — properly obtained, and the only status sealing is reachable from.
  - _Temporary_ — a placeholder (often a GO transfer) taking the slot for completion's sake, openly subject to upgrade when a better method appears.
  - _Unobtainable_ — no legitimate way to get it right now (event-locked raid mons, some mythicals). Logged rather than left blank, so the slot still carries a completion state. There's no specimen to describe, so it keeps no record: marking a mon unobtainable clears what was filled in.
- **Sealed** — a commitment above Caught, and only reachable from it: this exact specimen is the slot holder forever, even if a perfect one turns up later. Sealing needs every field it waits on answered (see _Per-mon records_) and a confirmation (in the Review, _Ctrl+Enter_ or its _Seal_ button seals at once), after which the mon is frozen: no form, no Release, just a read-only record whose location alone still changes (see _Moving a sealed mon_). A record sealed before a field it now waits on was added, or holding a value the app no longer writes, wears a warning pin naming what needs updating; nothing is changed for you, and updating one means unsealing it. Unsealing is a press-and-hold with a ring filling at the pointer, and drops it back to Caught.
- **Per-mon records** — _My Game_, _Origin Game_, _Language_, _OT_, _ID No._, _Been to Champions_, _Location_ (in HOME, in Champions, or which of your games it's sitting in), _Ball_, _Catch Date_ (typed as YYYY/MM/DD, three fields that keep the caret where you're editing, or picked from a calendar), _Gender_, _Nature_, _Nickname_, _Level_, _Trained_ and _Favorite_ (no, a favorite or a partner). Fields you have to look up start unanswered, and unanswered is distinct from "no": that's what gives the seal gate meaning. Fields whose options cover every state start at their baseline instead (_Been to Champions_ and _Favorite_ at no, _Trained_ at untrained, _Nickname_ unticked), so they never block a seal, short of a nickname ticked with no name; sealing waits on all the rest but _My Game_. A new mark in a HOME dex also starts _In HOME_, and elsewhere in a game, waiting to be told which. Wherever a record is written, a Cherish Ball makes the origin Mystery Gift, a nickname or a Mystery Gift origin floors _Favorite_ at favorite (a partner is left alone), and a species locked to one gender has its _Gender_ answered. Champions is two facts: having been there is permanent and survives the mon coming back, while _Location_ only offers _In Champions_ in a HOME dex once it has. _Release_ clears the slot and its record.
- **My Games** (nav) — each playthrough you own: game, language, the trainer name (OT) it stamps on everything caught there, and its ID No. (optional; it tells two files of one game apart), edited in place under _Edit List_ and put in order by dragging or _Alt+↑_/_↓_. One record names an origin, lists where a Pokémon can be sitting, and fills in OT: picking a game as a mon's _My Game_ answers origin game, language, OT and ID No. at once. It only works that way round: changing any of the four by hand changes that field alone, and _My Game_ then reads _Not my game_ unless the record still matches one of your games on all four, so a HOME gift keeps your HOME entry's OT and ID No. under the origin mark it came with. A HOME entry is a trainer, not a place, so it's never offered as somewhere a mon sits or moves to. Deleting a game says how many mons will need their location set again.
- **Champions roster** — Pokémon Champions takes only the Pokémon on its current roster, kept by hand in `data/champions.json` (see _Future-proofing_). Only those are asked _Been to Champions_ (it stays on show wherever it's already yes), and Champions is offered as a location or a place to move to only in a HOME dex, for a roster mon or one already there; the Ledger's batch bar offers it only when every selected mon qualifies. The roster only decides what's offered: it never changes or flags a stored record.
- **The Review** (_W_, or _Review one by one_ in the dex menu) — the main way to enter records, one mon at a time over the window: it runs through the mons the dex shows as it opens (its view, filters and search, in dex order), starting at the mon whose popover is open. The mon sits in the middle in a viewfinder with its types, every field of its record on a plate either side wired to it (where it came from and where it is on the left, the specimen on the right), a pip under it for each field sealing waits on, and the run listed beside it with how far each record has got (_L_ shows or hides the list; in a window under 1000px wide it lies over the mon). It's worked from the keyboard: _1_ _2_ _3_ mark Caught, Temporary or Unobtainable (by key, _3_ only marks an unmarked mon, so it can't wipe a record); _Enter_ goes from field to field through what's still to fill, opening each menu as it comes, and an answer picked from a menu by keyboard goes straight on; _Ctrl+Enter_ seals a ready record and stamps it, the mon staying up to be looked over, and again (or _E_) goes on; _Q_ and _E_ (or _←_ _→_) move between mons; _Escape_ leaves a field, then the list over the mon, then the Review, bringing the dex to the mon it ended on. A sealed record shows its values, its location still a dropdown, and a _Hold to unseal_ button (held with the pointer, or with _Enter_ or _Space_); an open one has _Release_. The box view's popover stays for quick edits.
- **Marking from the boxes** — a click marks an empty tile with the dex's default status and opens its popover beside the box, with the status and every field in two columns; a marked tile's click just opens it, and the next tile's click switches the popover over in place, so a box can be worked through tile by tile (a tile the popover has worked on stays in a filtered view until it closes). _Shift+click_ marks a tile without opening it (a marked one is left be), and _Alt+click_ opens one without marking it, for a look or its Bulbapedia and Serebii links. Holding a sealed tile unseals it, and _L_ over one opens its move menu. _D_ over a marked mon that isn't sealed (a tile or a Ledger row) releases it at a tap, erasing its record; a sealed one it leaves be. Right-click a tile (or a Ledger row, the name atop the popover, or a mon on the Timeline) to copy the Pokémon's name as shown, Japanese in Japanese and with its ♂ or ♀; a tag at the pointer says so. The page's text doesn't select otherwise, so a drag that strays off the scrollbar can't highlight half of it; fields and error messages still do.
- **Moving a sealed mon** — a sealed mon is the slot's holder for good, but where it is can still change: taken out to a game for raids, into Champions, back to HOME. Its _Location_ stays a dropdown in the popover and the Review, and _L_ over its tile or Ledger row opens the same places in a menu beside it: HOME, each of your games that can hold it (not one older than the species, a closed game without it, or one it would have had to move back into), and Champions in a HOME dex where the roster allows. The menu takes the arrows, _Home_/_End_, _Enter_ and _Space_ as the dropdowns do; _L_ again, _Escape_ or a click outside closes it. Moving one into Champions records that it's been there; nothing else on the record changes.
- **Display modes** — a sealed tile shows its record in one of two modes, picked in the theme menu or switched with _O_ (the flips fading out and back in around the switch). _Hexagon_, the default, flips its lower lines through three pages, each held 5.5s: the record's (language, level and the nature as a stat hexagon, over the badges: ball, origin mark, heart, gender, and the Champions mark or training), the trainer's (OT over catch date, the name flipping to the nickname) and the species' (its number and types). _Orbit_ is the ball alone, large on a flat ring, with up to five moons circling it (gender, heart, the game's mark, training, the nature's hexagon) and the nickname shown steadily in the name line; nothing flips. _Space_ holds the flips on the page on show: a pause button after the search lets them go, and a picker beside it (or _1_ _2_ _3_) holds another page. With nothing flipping (no seals yet, a checklist, the Ledger, Orbit), _Space_ scrolls as usual.
- **Types** — always on show: at the foot of every tile but a sealed one (a pair stacked) and on Hexagon's species page, named beside their glyphs in the Review, in the Ledger's _Type_ column, and as a filter and an Almanac ring each. The glyphs are Scarlet and Violet's symbols redrawn as clean shapes and evened out in size, each in its Scarlet and Violet colour moved only in lightness, per theme and in Light and Soft Dark apiece, until it reads on a sealed tile (WCAG's 3:1 for a graphic in Light, 4:1 in Soft Dark, on the fill and every location's wash). A regional form has its own (Alolan Vulpix is ice).
- **Still mode** (_M_, or the theme menu; kept through a restart) — for long sessions, or a dex left up on another screen: it stops everything that moves, with no exceptions. The flips hold where they are (the picker open, a pick landing at once, and neither _Space_ nor the pause button letting them go), Orbit's moons stand still, the boxes' shine goes and the meter is still, and every entrance, fade, slide and count-up lands at once, the Timeline's and the Almanac's included: the Timeline opens already settled, with no title card, and the Review, the Almanac and the other scenes open without theirs. The one motion left is a hold's fill, since that is the hold itself. The sealed washes and tints stay, so sealed mons still read as sealed.
- **The dex's header** — the dex's name, a menu of the _Timeline_, the _Almanac_, the Review, _Boxes_ or _Ledger_ and _Edit Dex_, with its tags after it; the search on the centre line, the flips' pause after it; a progress meter across the header's width, drawn in as the dex opens with its percentage counting alongside, then springing to each change; and the filters' row, with the counts (marked, to go, temporary, caught) at its end.
- **Views and filters** — one click switches the dex between _All_, _Missing_, _Temporary_, _Unsealed_ and _Incomplete_. The _Filters_ panel (_F_) narrows by generation, legendary/mythical, type and game dex (the games whose own Pokédex lists a species), which read the species and so work on missing slots too, and by origin game, language, OT, location, ball, nature, trained and favorite, which read the record and so count sealed mons alone (an unsealed record's fields are placeholders) and stand aside in the status views. Each value shows how many Pokémon it would leave under the search and the other filters, values within a filter combine with "or" and filters with "and", and active filters sit beside the panel as chips that clear with a click; _R_ clears them all. The search (_/_) takes a name in either language (in either kana), a nickname, or a number.
- **The Ledger** (_V_, or the dex menu) — the dex as a table in place of the boxes, the switch either way named on a small tab at the header's margin while the new view builds out of sight: number, name, types, status (a lock when sealed, the warning pin), location, origin game, ball, language, OT, nickname, catch date and level, the less needed columns dropping out of a narrower window. The search, the view and the filters narrow it as they do the boxes, and a row does what its tile does. A column's head sorts by it (again for the other way, a third time back to dex order), and the rows group by box, generation, type, status, origin game, ball, nature, location, language, OT or year caught, each group under a head with its counts and a bar of its statuses, folding away when pressed; the layout, the sort and the grouping are kept across dexes and restarts. A row's box or _Ctrl+click_ selects it, _Shift+click_ a run, _Ctrl+A_ every row shown, and _Escape_ lets them go. A selection brings up a batch bar that sets a status on every unsealed one (marking the unmarked), sets one field (_My Game_, origin game, language, OT, ball, catch date, level, trained, favorite or location) on every marked record that isn't sealed (sealed ones only move), and seals the ready ones or releases them, each after a confirmation.
- **Scenes** — whole-window views over the dex: the Review, the _Timeline_ (_T_), the _Almanac_ (_A_), _Journeys_ (_J_), _Red Pen_ (_P_) and _Atlas_ (_G_), the last three by key alone. Each opens on a title card (its name slammed onto a slash of the accent, the dex's name sliding in under it; any key or a click skips it) and closes with _Escape_. A press in one hands the dex a filter or a mon: a bar or value in the Almanac, or a strand or stop in Journeys, sets the dex's filters to what it counts, while a mon pressed on the Timeline, among the Almanac's milestones and latest catches, in Red Pen or on the Atlas is brought into view with its popover open, the search and filters cleared first if they'd hide it. The scene stays up until the dex has drawn the new view whole, then goes in one frame, so the old view never flashes. A checklist, which keeps no records, has no Review, Timeline, Journeys or Red Pen.
- **Timeline** (_T_) — the dex's catches as a scene of their own: after the title card, every marked mon drops onto the day it was caught, oldest first, the bar counting each in as it lands. Each day is one stack, the days of a month side by side with their dates under them, so a stack's height is that day's count; long quiet stretches fold into "3 years later" cards, and mons with no catch date go on a "???" pile of their own. It opens fitted to its tallest stack (never under 35%, scrolling up past that) and zooms like a map: _Ctrl+scroll_ (or a pinch) about the pointer, _+_ / _−_ or the bar's buttons, _0_, _Z_ or the zoom level to fit again; drag to pan, scroll to go along it, _Shift+scroll_ to go up the stacks. Hover a mon for its name, nickname, date and where it came from (game, language and OT, with a pad when that's one of your games); click it to go to its tile. The bar names the busiest month and replays the entrance.
- **Almanac** (_A_) — the dex counted every way its records allow: how much is marked and how, by generation, and by type (a ring each in its type's colour); then every value its sealed records hold, packed into columns (where they came from, trainers, balls, favorites and partners, nicknames, Mystery Gifts, training, legendaries and mythicals, where the sealed ones are now, languages); then catches by month with the busiest named, the latest catches, and milestones along a line (the 1st catch, the 10th, the 50th… and the latest). Each number counts up with the ring or bar that draws it, the two landing together. A row, ring or value pressed filters the dex to it; a milestone or a latest catch goes to its tile.
- **Journeys** (_J_) — the master set's sealed mons as strands from the game each was caught in to the generation its slot belongs to, games and generations newest at the top, each strand as thick as its mons and in its game's colour, a gold top edge marking the share that has been to Champions. A toggle moves every strand over to where the mons are now (HOME, Champions, or out in one of your games). The busiest strands are listed beside the chart, and hovering a strand or a stop shows its mons. Pressing a strand, a game, a generation or a place filters the dex to it as a layer: a _Journeys_ tab before the filter chips, or _Escape_, goes back to Journeys as it was left, the dex's own filters and search restored, until either is changed by hand.
- **Red Pen** (_P_) — the dex's sealed records read over as a teacher marks a paper, in red ink on ruled paper: a ✗ for what can't be right (a catch dated in the future or before its game came out, a species its game can't have, a ball or a language the game never had, a file of yours that can't hold the mon in it, an ID No. that doesn't fit its generation or isn't the file its OT is, a name longer than its game took) and a △ for what's worth a second look (a mon its game's dex doesn't list, a Strange Ball, your name with an ID No. none of your files has or in a language you have no file of, a name its language's games can't type), the fields each is about ringed and a note in the margin. One trainer's name written several ways is listed apart, and a record under a HOME entry's ID No. is read as a HOME gift, whatever origin mark it carries. The sheet is marked out of 100, the share of sealed records with no ✗, and a perfect one gets はなまる; unsealed drafts can be read too, but are never scored. A row opens the Review on every flagged mon from it; _Show in Dex_ goes to its tile.
- **Atlas** (_G_) — every one of your dexes as a strip along the national dex, a column per species coloured for how it stands there (sealed, caught, temporary, unobtainable, missing, or not in that dex), the generations marked under them. The pointer (or _←_ _→_, _Shift_ for ten) runs a line down every strip at once, with a loupe saying how that species stands in each dex; pressed (or _Enter_), it goes to its tile in the open dex. Under the strips: what's missing here but marked in another dex, and what's still missing from two or more, where one catch could fill them all.
- **Keyboard Shortcuts** (_H_) — every key and gesture on one sheet, grouped by where it works (the dex, a tile or row, the Ledger, the Review, the Timeline, anywhere), with its keys as caps; _H_ again, _Escape_ or a click outside closes it, a checklist's leaves out what it hasn't got, and macOS shows ⌘ and ⌥. _Escape_ anywhere closes what's open, or goes back to Journeys from a filter it set. The dex's single-key shortcuts stay quiet while typing, with a menu open, under a modal or a scene, or with a modifier held.
- **Multiple personal dexes** — each is its own instance of a catalog dex with independent progress, opened, created and reordered (dragged, or _Alt+↑_/_↓_) on the landing page, each wearing its game's colour down its spine; the logo returns there. Each dex can define _defaults_ for status, _My Game_, ball and trained (_Edit Dex_, in the dex's menu), applied whenever a mon is newly marked: e.g. a regional living dex where every catch comes from the same cartridge. Leave a default unset where the dex is a wildcard and you want to fill it by hand; OT takes none, coming from the game picked.
- **Checklist dexes** — a dex can instead be created as a checklist: statuses only, with no records, defaults or popover. A click checks a tile as caught and hovering offers a button for each other status; a marked tile ignores clicks and is cleared by holding it (or a tap of _D_ over it). A caught check wears the sealed look without its meaning; the Review, the Timeline, Journeys, Red Pen, the flips, _Alt+click_ and _L_ aren't there, and the views keep to _All_, _Missing_ and _Temporary_ and the filters to the species. The choice is made at creation and is permanent.
- **Fit and zoom** — the boxes scale themselves to the window so two span it, or one where two would shrink below 80% (a narrow window shows one box at a readable size rather than two at half), never enlarged past what a whole row can show, with the first row a fixed gap under the header; any room left over fills with more boxes across. _Ctrl+scroll_ (or _Ctrl+=_ / _Ctrl+−_, _Ctrl+0_ or _Z_ to reset; _Cmd_ on macOS) zooms the boxes on top of that fit, out through each count of boxes across to exactly four and in to 200%, and the level is remembered; the nav and the header keep their size and place, the header as wide as the boxes at 100%. The Review, the Almanac, Journeys, Red Pen and Atlas are laid out for a 1400px window and scale up to a wider one. A window too narrow for one box (750px) shows a plain list instead. _F11_ toggles fullscreen.
- **Title bar** — on Windows the nav is the window's title bar: drag it to move the window and double-click it to maximize. Minimize, maximize and close sit at its right end in the theme's colours, and the nav's icons keep clear of them. macOS keeps its standard title bar.
- **Localization** — the whole UI (including Pokémon names) toggles between English and Japanese from the nav, while nicknames and OTs are set by their own script whatever the UI language. Small Japanese text (labels, menu items, notes; 12px and under) is set a pixel larger than the English, where kanji and kana don't read as well as Latin capitals.
- **Themes and display** (the nav's theme menu, under the palette icon) — eleven themes: Apricot (the default), Matcha, Harbor, Wisteria and Graphite, and six with a look of their own, Pokédex, Game Boy, Midnight, Ink, HOME and Ember; each comes in Light and _Soft Dark_, a full palette of its own rather than a darkened copy. Below them are the display mode (_Hexagon Display_, _Orbit Display_), _Location Tints_ and _Still Mode_. With tints on, a sealed mon away from where its dex keeps them is tinted for where it is (gold in Champions; rose out in one of your games, in a HOME dex; blue in HOME, in a game's), while one where the dex keeps them wears the plain sealed wash; off, the away ones wear it too. The theme, Soft Dark, the tints and still mode restyle the dex without redrawing a tile.

## Data Persistence

All state lives on this machine; there is no server or account.

- **Desktop (Electron)** — a single JSON file, `dex_data.json`, in the per-user app data directory (e.g. `%APPDATA%/tsukamae` on Windows). The main process holds a copy of the tracker: a mark, an edit, a move or a release sends just the records it changed through the preload bridge, which it merges in (dex-level changes and imports send the whole state), and it coalesces rapid changes into debounced, atomic writes (temp file flushed to disk, then renamed over the old one, retried while antivirus or a backup tool holds it) that run strictly in order, and quitting waits for the last one, so progress can't be corrupted or lost mid-session. A save that can't reach the disk is reported in a banner until one does. A file that can't be read, or isn't tracker data, is reported and left alone rather than treated as empty; the error screen can still import an export in its place, which copies it aside to `dex_data.unreadable.json` first (numbered if there's one already). Only one copy of the app runs at a time, so two can never race each other's writes. The window's size and position sit beside it in `window-state.json`.
- **Preferences** — the language, the theme and its switches, the zoom, and the boxes-or-Ledger choice with its sort and grouping live in the window's own storage rather than `dex_data.json`, so they stay with the machine and an export doesn't carry them.
- **Browser dev** (`yarn start`) — the same state falls back to `localStorage`. `yarn start:fresh` keeps everything in the tab's session storage only, and `yarn electron:dev:fresh` runs the desktop app on a throwaway profile, so testing never touches real data.
- **Export / Import** — the nav's data menu (the gear) downloads the dexes and My Games as a JSON snapshot and restores from one (import replaces all current state, and refuses anything that isn't a tsukamae 2.0 export). This is also the upgrade path across dataset regenerations.

## Development

Requires Node (see `.node-version`) and Yarn. Use **Yarn, not npm** — the committed lockfile is `yarn.lock`, and some type packages (e.g. `@types/react`) are peer dependencies that npm 7+ silently auto-installs but Yarn does not. They are pinned explicitly in `package.json`, so `yarn install` gives a complete, type-checkable tree.

```bash
yarn install

# Desktop app (webpack dev server + Electron, hot reload; F12 devtools, Ctrl+R reload,
# Ctrl+Shift+P to see it as a production build has it, without the dev-only Theme Preview)
yarn electron:dev

# Browser-only dev (persistence falls back to localStorage)
yarn start # http://localhost:9898

# Build a Windows installer (output in dist/; runs yarn lint:all first)
yarn electron:build

# Lint, typecheck and the theme audit
yarn lint:all
```

## Future-proofing

The dex structure is a static snapshot in `data/` — `dexes/<key>/` (one directory per catalog dex: `meta.json` + `pokemon.json`), `games.json` (origin-game options), generated once from the live pokedextracker API and PokéAPI, and the hand-maintained `languages.json`, `balls.json` and `champions.json` — all committed; the app never fetches anything. Each Pokémon record keeps only what the app reads (upstream's nested game family is reduced to its generation as it's written):

```bash
yarn dataset          # scripts/generate-dataset.mjs + scripts/enrich-species.mjs + scripts/enrich-types.mjs
yarn dataset:species  # re-attach Japanese names, legendary/mythical class and gender locks only
yarn dataset:types    # re-attach each slot's types (a regional form's own) from PokéAPI's data tables
```

When a new generation arrives:

1. Export Data from the nav's gear menu (belt and braces — regeneration only touches `data/`, never personal progress, which lives outside the repo entirely).
2. Run `yarn dataset`, and add any new dex to `DEX_MANIFEST` in `scripts/generate-dataset.mjs` (or `POKEAPI_DEX_MANIFEST`, plus a `SYNTHETIC_GAMES` entry for a game pokedextracker lacks), and to the imports and `DEX_CATALOG` in `app/utils/local-data.ts` (with `GAME_NAME_OVERRIDES` for a paired release). The Generation III to V games pokedextracker lacks (FireRed and LeafGreen through Black 2 and White 2) are in the script's `OLDER_ORIGINS`, so a rerun keeps them in `data/games.json`.
3. A new game family also wants `SEREBII_LINKS` (`PokemonPopover.tsx`, which otherwise falls back to the newest dex), `ORIGIN_MARK_SPRITES` (`seal-faces.tsx`) with `MARKS` in `scripts/fetch-origin-marks.mjs` (`yarn sprites:marks`), its generation and release day in `GAME_FACTS` (`app/utils/game-facts.ts`, which Red Pen and the move menus read), a colour in `app/utils/game-colors.ts`, an entry in `GAME_DEXES` (`app/components/pages/Tracker/filters.ts`) for the _Game Dex_ filter, and Japanese names in `JA_ORIGIN_GAMES`, `JA_CATALOG_GAMES`, `JA_DEX_TYPES`, `JA_CATALOG_DEX_NAMES` and `JA_BOX_NAMES` (`app/i18n/names.ts`); a new generation wants its region's name for Journeys (`journeys.region.N` in `app/i18n/translations.ts`, both languages). Anything left out falls back to English, no mark, no colour or no check.
4. Update the sprite sheet (`public/pokesprite-v12.png` + `app/styles/pokesprite.scss`) from upstream, then run `yarn sprites:fit`, which rewrites `app/styles/pokesprite-fit.scss` so the tallest sprites still fit a tile.
5. New non-cartridge origins go in `EXTRA_ORIGINS` in `scripts/generate-dataset.mjs` as well as `data/games.json` (a rerun rewrites the file from the script), plus a Japanese name in `app/i18n/names.ts`.

Pokémon Champions' roster is kept by hand in `data/champions.json`, read by `app/utils/champions.ts`; nothing fetches it. Its `season`, `updated` and `source` say which regulation it holds (Regulation M-C, from Serebii's roster page), and `pokemon` lists one entry per Pokémon that can go there, by `national` number with a `name` for reading; a regional form (alola, galar, hisui, paldea) is an entry of its own with a `form`, while any other form, a Gigantamax slot included, goes with its species. When a season adds Pokémon, append them and update those three fields. Nothing is removed: the roster only decides what's offered, never what a record says.

## Architecture

React 18 + TypeScript bundled by webpack; Sass modules; Electron as a thin desktop shell. No router, no server, no accounts — the view is derived from which dex is active.

```
app/
├── components/
│   ├── library/                # Shared pieces: nav, modals (Edit Dex, My Games), dropdown, field controls
│   └── pages/
│       ├── Landing.tsx         # Dex list (create, reorder, open)
│       ├── Tracker/            # The dex page:
│       │   ├── index.tsx       #   header, boxes or Ledger, popover and scenes, their keys, and handOff
│       │   ├── SearchBar.tsx   #   the header (DexMenu, the flips' pause, DexMeter, FilterBar)
│       │   ├── Dex.tsx         #   the boxes (Box, Pokemon), search results or the Ledger; L and D
│       │   ├── Ledger.tsx      #   the list view, with ledger-model.ts and BatchBar.tsx
│       │   ├── FlipStrips.tsx  #   sealed tiles' pages (face-pages.tsx) on one clock (use-flip-clock.ts)
│       │   ├── Review.tsx      #   W
│       │   ├── Timeline.tsx    #   T, laid out by timeline-layout.ts
│       │   ├── Almanac.tsx     #   A, counted by almanac-data.ts
│       │   ├── Scene.tsx       #   the shell Journeys, Red Pen and Atlas share
│       │   ├── Journeys.tsx    #   J, drawn from journeys-model.ts
│       │   ├── RedPen.tsx      #   P, drawn from redpen-model.ts
│       │   ├── Atlas.tsx       #   G, drawn from atlas-model.ts
```

Key mechanics:

- **State** — the bundled catalog (`utils/local-data.ts`) is structure; a personal dex is a catalog reference plus a sparse progress map, with My Games beside the dexes. One in-memory app state is mutated synchronously and mirrored to disk write-behind; tiles update optimistically from the same change.
- **Field registry** — `utils/capture-fields.ts` declares every field once (its kind, whether sealing waits on it, whether a dex can default it, its baseline), and the popover, the Review's plates, the batch bar, the seal gate (with the _Incomplete_ view and the warning pin) and the dex defaults all read it; the cross-field rules (`withFieldInvariants`) run wherever a record is written.
- **Color system** — a theme is five hues and a chroma scale (`palette/themes.ts`), and the six with a look of their own add tones that step off the shared lightness ladder; every colour in both Light and Soft Dark is computed from them in OKLCH and written onto `:root` as role tokens (`paper`, `chrome`, `overlay`, `tile-caught`, `on-tile`…) before the first render. Text roles are solved to their WCAG target, the location washes per theme so each still reads as its colour, and `yarn lint:themes` audits every theme × mode for contrast and for statuses that blur together. The type glyphs' colours (`type-*`) are solved the same way, from each type's Scarlet and Violet colour, and audited on a sealed tile's lower third. The dev-only Theme Preview (flask button, TESTING) shows every theme in both modes at once, with a sheet of all eighteen glyphs beside each on the tile and every location's wash (each glyph's ratio on hover); _Ctrl+scroll_ zooms it about the pointer, a drag pans it and _Ctrl+0_ resets it.
- **i18n** — a flat key→string dictionary per locale; `ja` is typed against `en`'s keys so the dictionaries cannot drift.
- **Flips** — a sealed tile's pages aren't drawn by the tile. Each row of boxes gets an overlay (`FlipStrips`) with one clipped window per tile row and line (the name, and the lower block of number line and badges, a page at a time), whose faces slide as a single composited strip, and every strip runs one keyframe cycle, built per display mode from its page count (`styles/tile-faces.scss`), on the compositor from a shared start time (`use-flip-clock.ts`). A flip costs the main thread nothing, and a strip that appears later joins in step. Orbit's moons circle as one group per row and slot, by transform alone. The overlays skip rendering offscreen on their own and paint after every box's tiles: Chromium's layer assignment slows with the square of composited layers painted between boxes, which is what made zoomed-out views lag.
- **Scenes and hand-off** — the Timeline, the Review and the Almanac are scenes of their own, and Journeys, Red Pen and Atlas share `Scene.tsx`'s shell (the bar, the title card, _Escape_, the scale to a wide window); the figures they draw come from pure functions of the captures (`*-model.ts`, `almanac-data.ts`, `timeline-layout.ts`). Each opens on `TitleCard.tsx`'s card and holds its motion until its frames come steadily (`useCalmStart`), as a scene's first paint stalls even composited animation. A press that sends the user to the dex goes through `handOff` (`Tracker/index.tsx`): the scene stays up while the dex still shows its last view (its filters render deferred, `data-stale`) or a grid being rebuilt (`data-hidden`), for 1.5s at most, then everything closing goes in one frame. A view that builds a few rows or boxes a frame (the Ledger switch, a display mode's fade) waits on `whenSettled` (`utils/when-settled.ts`), which resolves once nothing has been added or removed for three frames.
- **Still mode** — `data-still` on the root: past each sheet's own rules, one rule plays every animation and transition in no time, so an entrance lands on its last frame (its `animationend` still firing) and a new animation is covered before it has a rule of its own; only a hold's ring keeps its duration (`--hold`). What script drives (the flip clock, the meter's count, the title cards, the Timeline's and the Almanac's entrances) checks the setting itself.
